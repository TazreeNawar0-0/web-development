About Cats:

• Domestic cats share about 95.6% of their genetic makeup with tigers. Just like human fingerprints, every cat has a completely unique pattern of ridges on their nose. Cats have 32 muscles in each ear, allowing them to rotate their ears 180 degrees to pinpoint sounds. Cats have only about 473 taste buds (compared to 9,000 in humans) and cannot taste sweet flavors. A cat's whiskers are generally as wide as their body and contain up to 200,000 sensory receptors. Cats have between 230 and 250 bones in their skeletons, which is more than humans have.

Physical Abilities:

A cat can jump up to 6 times its own length or height in a single bound. Over short distances, a healthy house cat can sprint at speeds up to 30 mph. Cats do not get human-style tooth decay because their pointed teeth leave less room for trapped food, though they can develop other dental diseases. Cats spend roughly 70% of their lives sleeping, which usually adds up to 13 to 16 hours a day.

History & Behavior:

The oldest known pet cat grave was discovered in Cyprus and dates back about 9,500 years. Adult cats rarely meow at other cats; they developed the meow specifically to communicate with people. A cat's purr vibrates at a frequency of 25 to 150 Hz, which is a range that scientists note can promote tissue and bone repair. Studies show that male cats tend to be left-pawed, while female cats usually favor their right paw.

What They Eat:

• Mice, voles, and young rats make up the largest portion of a wild cat's diet because they are abundant and easy to catch according to the Essential Foods Guide. • Birds: Ground-nesting and small perching birds are regularly hunted. • Reptiles & Amphibians: Lizards, snakes, and frogs are frequent catches. • Insects: Grasshoppers, beetles, and other large bugs provide a small protein and nutrient supplement. • Small Mammals: Young rabbits or small hares are common when available.
